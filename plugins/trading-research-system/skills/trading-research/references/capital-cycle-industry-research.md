# Capital Cycle Industry Research

Use this reference when an industry, theme, or research source argues from a
supply-side capital cycle rather than from demand narrative alone. It is
especially useful for AI infrastructure, semiconductors, data centers, power,
optical networking, memory, and other capital-intensive technology cycles.

This framework can change research priority, strategy posture, and the
Cross-Section Candidate Pool. It is not a setup trigger and must not promote a
ticker directly into an intraday or weekly setup without macro/financial
support, price structure, risk context, and user profile fit.

## Source Boundary

Treat videos, podcasts, newsletters, social posts, and similar material as `S3
research / opinion` unless their claims are independently verified. They are
useful for thesis generation, counter-thesis discovery, and research questions;
they are not proof of current facts.

When a capital-cycle source makes material claims, create a Primary
Verification Queue before changing the Active Market Plan:

- S0/S1 company filings, earnings releases, IR decks, transcripts, and market
  data for capex, revenue, utilization, margin, backlog, and pricing claims.
- S0/S1 macro and financial-condition data for rates, liquidity, credit, and
  funding conditions.
- S2 reputable financial media only as reported context or lead generation.
- S3 research / opinion only as thesis input until verified.

## Core Thesis

The operating rule is: demand can be noisy. It is often narrative-driven and
temporarily supported by incentives or financing loops. Supply-side facts are
harder to fake because they require capital committed upfront, assets built,
depreciation, financing, and capacity that must later earn a return.

The capital cycle asks whether capital is entering an industry faster than
sustainable end demand and return on invested capital can absorb.

## Capital Cycle Phases

| Phase | Read | What To Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flood | New high-return story attracts capital and new entrants. | rising capex, easy funding, new projects, aggressive capacity additions, valuation expansion |
| Boom | Supply, competition, and expectations grow faster than verified demand. | falling pricing power, customer concentration, utilization risk, rising sales/marketing intensity, margin pressure |
| Collapse | Excess supply compresses returns and forces failures or consolidation. | price cuts, cancelled projects, inventory, debt stress, bankruptcies, estimate cuts, valuation compression |
| Inheritance | Survivors or second movers acquire/use assets cheaply after capital exits. | asset sales, consolidation, reduced competition, lower replacement cost, improved future returns |

## Supply-Side Cycle Check

Use this checklist inside `Industry/Sector Strength` or as a standalone compact
block in weekly or macro-equity research.

| Field | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Capital Cycle Phase | Is the theme in Flood, Boom, Collapse, or Inheritance? What evidence would change that phase? |
| Builders | Who is funding and building supply: hyperscaler, chip vendor, memory supplier, data center, power utility, optical/networking supplier, or software company? |
| Economic Owner | Who records revenue today, and who carries capex, depreciation, lease obligations, financing risk, or utilization risk? |
| End Demand Quality | Is demand verified end demand, pre-committed customer demand, vendor financing, circular financing, or narrative demand? |
| Capacity | Are capacity additions accelerating faster than utilization, pricing, or customer budgets? |
| Pricing Power | Are ASPs, margins, renewal terms, contract prices, or backlog quality improving or deteriorating? |
| Funding Conditions | Do higher yields, tighter credit, weak equity issuance, or customer financing constraints pressure the cycle? |
| Competition | Are new entrants or second-source alternatives compressing returns? |
| Inheritance Candidate | Who could inherit cheap assets, customer relationships, data center capacity, GPU capacity, fiber, power access, or software distribution after the cycle resets? |

## AI Infrastructure Lens

For AI infrastructure and semiconductor themes, track these supply-side
indicators before accepting a bullish or bearish narrative:

- hyperscaler and AI lab capex intensity;
- data center capacity additions, power access, cooling, and grid constraints;
- GPU, accelerator, memory, HBM, NAND, optical networking, switch, and power
  equipment order trends;
- utilization, backlog quality, cancellation risk, and customer concentration;
- vendor financing, circular financing, strategic investments, prepayments, and
  revenue that depends on customer financing;
- depreciation, lease commitments, free cash flow, gross margin, and operating
  leverage;
- software/application sales efficiency, sales/marketing intensity, retention,
  and pricing;
- guidance sensitivity when a small miss or cautious comment causes large
  market-cap loss;
- sector-relative price action for semiconductors, AI hardware, AI software,
  power, optical networking, and memory.

## Output Blocks

When this framework is material, include these blocks:

### Supply-Side Cycle Check

- `phase`: Flood / Boom / Collapse / Inheritance / unclear
- `supply_read`: concise supply-side read
- `demand_quality`: verified end demand / financed demand / circular demand /
  narrative demand / unclear
- `pricing_power`: improving / stable / deteriorating / unclear
- `funding_pressure`: low / medium / high / unclear
- `inheritance_candidates`: tickers, industries, or asset owners to watch
- `evidence`: source-prioritized facts already verified
- `next_check`: the next primary source, data series, or earnings call item

### Primary Verification Queue

List only checks that can change the plan. Examples:

- latest company capex, depreciation, backlog, revenue quality, and margin data;
- earnings call comments on pricing, utilization, capacity, customer financing,
  customer concentration, and cancellations;
- industry capacity additions and supply/demand balance;
- rates, credit spreads, and funding conditions;
- current relative strength and price-action confirmation.

### Trade Plan Preparation Impact

Translate the research into planning language:

- `Industry/Sector Strength`: upgrade, downgrade, or watch-only based on
  supply-side evidence.
- `Company Thesis Check`: separate sellers of picks-and-shovels revenue from
  companies carrying capacity, utilization, or financing risk.
- `Cross-Section Candidate Pool`: add only when the next check is concrete.
- `Setup Pool`: do not promote directly. Require higher-timeframe regime,
  price structure, trigger zone, invalidation, and portfolio risk review.

## Guardrails

- Do not treat a compelling capital-cycle story as a timing signal.
- Do not rely on one video, podcast, or article without verification.
- Do not overfit historical analogies. Railways, fiber, shale, and AI
  infrastructure can rhyme without matching exactly.
- Do not say a bubble is confirmed unless market data, financing conditions,
  pricing, utilization, and company facts support that conclusion.
- Do not downgrade a theme-core pool only because capital is flooding in; use
  the framework to define what must be verified, what would invalidate the
  thesis, and where the eventual inheritance candidates may be.
